Email 2 - Day 2

Subject: The first listing I lost, and what I changed

Pre-header: And the order I built behind it

{First name},

Day 0 was the score and the order of operations. Today is a specific story.

The first listing I lost as a newer agent was the one I most wanted to win. The seller had a clear timeline, a clean comparable, and a price band the market was actively absorbing. I prepared the presentation. I walked in. I lost it.

What I changed after that loss matters more than the loss itself. Here is the order:

Step one. I rebuilt the listing-presentation script from the comparable-sales workflow up. The presentation I had walked in with was 80% market-puff and 20% specific comp work. The new one was 80% specific comp work and 20% strategy framing. The conversion rate moved measurably inside three reps.

Step two. I added an objection-handling sheet to the back of the playbook. Every objection that came up in the first 10 listing presentations went into the sheet with a response. The sheet was alive - it got rewritten every quarter for the first two years.

Step three. I built a comparable-sales workflow that I could run inside 60 minutes from MLS pull to printable comps document. Speed mattered because most of the strongest sellers were also talking to other agents who could not turn the comps around inside the same window.

Step four. I started tracking presentation-to-listing-agreement conversion as a real number, weekly. The number sat lower than I wanted for the first six months. It moved once I started treating the presentation as a system with specific inputs, not as a single performance.

Step five. I added the local-market and military-relocation-lane specifics to the presentation. The MRP credential came later, but the operational awareness of the 757 base cycle was a differentiator from the start.

The lesson, in one sentence: the order of operations matters more than the polish of any single touchpoint.

The cohort I am building applies this directly. Track A walks the listing-presentation playbook (and the rest of the order) in 8 weeks with weekly live calls and a 1:1 plan review at the end. If your readiness score put you below 50, Track A is the fit.

More on Day 5.

Pre-header: The honest career arc most agents do not publish

{First name},

People ask what the career arc actually was. They expect a viral-takeoff story or a "I closed 30 deals in year one" story. It was neither.

Here is the honest arc, in years:

Year one and two. Licensed, working full-time, building the lead-gen base. Most months were single-digit transactions. The buyer side was 70% of the volume, the listing side was 30%. The listing-presentation conversion rate was lower than I wanted. The sphere referrals were starting to come in but they were not the dominant source.

The MRP-credentialed military-relocation lane started being a measurable percentage of the volume. The sphere referrals were stronger.

Year four and five. Volume scaled. The listing side moved from 30% of the book to closer to 50%. The military-relocation lane held its share. The decision to start FLOH Realty Group as a team came toward the end of this window - the question that broke open was "if I am leaving production on the table because I am the only producer, what does the operational answer look like."

Year six and seven. FLOH Realty Group in build. First agents hired. Splits-and-structure decisions made (and remade). The Local President role at Creed Realty was a parallel-track decision - the WCR leadership ladder followed.

Year eight. The team is operating. The credential stack is in place. The IG audience has grown to where the next leverage layer is the productised education lane - which is the cohort I am now teaching.

Email 4 - Day 8

Subject: The lead-gen order most VA + NC agents get wrong

Pre-header: Plus the three lead-gen lanes I would prioritise, and the ones I would deprioritise

{First name},

I look at a lot of "agent lead-gen" content. Most of it has the same two mistakes:

One. It lists dozens of lead-gen tactics without an order of operations. The reader picks the one that sounds easiest (cold-call scripts, paid Zillow leads, vague "social media presence") and skips the sphere audit, the past-client referral system, and the niche-calendar build.

Two. It treats all lead-gen sources as interchangeable. They are not. The sphere is the highest-quality lead source for most agents. Paid lead-gen platforms can fill the pipe but the conversion is typically lower than sphere. Niche-specialty lead-gen (MRP, luxury, first-time-buyer) sits in between on quality and volume. Treating them as equivalent is the source of the "I tried six lead-gen tactics and gave up on five of them" loop.

Here is the framework I run:

  • Tier one - sphere foundation. Past clients, family, friends, the agents you trained with, the local-board contacts. The sphere is the highest-conversion lead source for almost every agent in years 1-3. The tier-one work is the systematic monthly touch on the sphere, not the cold-call script.
  • Tier two - niche-specialty lead-gen. Military-relocation in the 757 (MRP-credentialed), first-time-buyer, luxury, move-up. Pick one. Build the calendar and the content cadence behind it. Earn the first niche referral.
  • Tier three - content-driven inbound. IG reels + LinkedIn + Facebook in the "Real Take" register, sized for the audience you want to attract. Slower lever, compounds across years. Tier three feeds tier one (sphere expansion) and tier two (niche-credential reinforcement).
  • Tier four - paid lead-gen platforms. Zillow, Realtor.com, Google PPC. Capital-heavy, conversion-lower-per-lead. Only after tiers one through three are running.

The two lead-gen categories most listicles include that I would deprioritise:

  • Door-knocking as a primary strategy. The operator-input-to-conversion ratio is poor for most agents in the 757 metro. Not for everyone, but rarely the right tier-one first lane.
  • Cold-calling FSBO and expired listings as the foundation. The conversion rate is real but the daily-volume requirement is high enough that it competes with every other tier-one lane for the same hours.

The cohort walks the tier framework in weeks 1 through 4 of Track A, applied to your specific geography, niche, and operator inputs.

It is the kind of structural fix that does not look impressive in a short-form video but moves the lead-flow more than any single tactic.
